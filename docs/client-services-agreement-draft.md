# Elison Inc. — Draft Client Services Agreement and SOW Framework

> **DRAFT FOR ATTORNEY REVIEW. NOT AN OFFER, NOT EXECUTED, NOT A WEBSITE CLICKWRAP.**
> Resolve legal entity name, address, jurisdiction, state licensing/taxes, enforceable clauses, local law, and contract-signing workflow with counsel before using. Do not assume this draft protects either party without informed acceptance and signatures.

## Master services agreement — proposed sections

### 1. Parties and authority
**Provider:** [Exact legal entity], doing business as Elison Inc. where applicable, [registered address].
**Client:** [Full legal name], [address].
**Effective date:** [date].
Both signatories confirm authority to bind the named parties. Do not use a trade name alone if the actual entity is different.

### 2. Scope and changes
Provider will perform only the services and deliverables in an attached, accepted Statement of Work (SOW). Any change to scope, versions, revision count, licenses, cost, timeline, integrations or deliverables requires a mutually approved written change order. Informal suggestions or a social-media message are not automatically added to the contracted scope.

### 3. Cooperation and approvals
Client provides accurate project information, required materials, brand guidance, authorized account access and feedback by the milestones stated in the SOW. Each SOW identifies one client decision-maker who can approve deliverables and scope changes. Provider documents client approvals and identifies dependencies or delays that materially affect delivery.

### 4. Fees, invoices and cancellation
Each SOW specifies fee type, deposit, payment milestones, permitted payment methods, invoice due dates, taxes, third-party costs, and what is due if the project is stopped. Cancellation, rescheduling and refunds must be clear **before** payment and compliant with applicable law. Avoid blanket “nonrefundable under all circumstances” language. The $200 consultation is a separate one-time service and does not automatically book a meeting.

### 5. Delivery, review and corrections
The SOW states formats, acceptance criteria, review windows, included revisions and error-correction procedures. Additional creative direction, new features, extra aspect ratios, late client changes or requests beyond the included revisions require approval of fees and time impacts. Do not treat silence as acceptance unless counsel approves a fair and lawful method.

### 6. Intellectual property and source assets
- **Client-provided material:** Client retains ownership and grants Provider the rights needed to work with it, subject to any expressly agreed limitations. Client confirms it has permission to provide and use the materials.
- **Provider background materials:** Provider retains its pre-existing technologies, scripts, workflows, templates, methods and production infrastructure, including Eclipse Studio. No sale, assignment, exclusivity or general access is implied.
- **Commissioned deliverables:** Ownership or the precise scope of a license, permitted media, territory, term, exclusivity and timing of transfer must be specified in the SOW. Any rights transfer must account for third-party licenses and valid payment conditions.
- **Third-party material:** The SOW identifies stock footage, fonts, music, software libraries, models, voice services and other assets governed by separate licenses. Do not promise rights broader than the actual license grants.
- **Source files:** Whether code, raw footage, editable project files, prompts, model weights, design files or other underlying sources are included must be explicitly stated; otherwise do not imply they are part of final delivery.

### 7. AI-assisted production and truthful advertising
State whether generative AI or synthetic imagery/audio will be used, its intended role, vendor terms or data restrictions, review/approval process, and risks that matter to the intended use. Client and Provider should not impersonate real people, use voices or likenesses without proper consent, or publish deceptive advertising claims. Client approval of final brand claims and any necessary endorsements, consent releases or regulatory disclosures must be documented. Avoid guaranteed copyright/registrability assertions for generative output without legal review.

### 8. Privacy, confidentiality and security
Define confidential information, permitted recipients, reasonable protection, exclusions, required disclosures, return/deletion procedures and security-incident notice expectations. When handling personal customer data, recordings or regulated records, add a separate data processing/security schedule appropriate to the project. Require secure credential exchange and least-privilege access; no secrets in GitHub repositories or public intake forms. If a confidentiality agreement is required before sharing, execute it **before** transmitting sensitive files.

### 9. Publicity and portfolio permission
Provide an explicit client choice for whether finished deliverables and the client's name/logo can be shown in portfolios, announcements, testimonials and case studies, and whether work must remain confidential until a specified release date. Do not assume payment grants portfolio publicity rights.

### 10. Services, expectations and limitations
The SOW identifies reasonable project warranties or corrective support where offered, any excluded ongoing support, and third-party service dependencies. Do not guarantee business outcomes such as sales, lead volume, virality, search ranking, ad approval or continual third-party availability. Have counsel tailor any liability limitations, indemnities, exclusions, dispute procedures, governing law, insurance and remedies to applicable law and the client type; **do not copy generic boilerplate or erase mandatory consumer rights**.

### 11. Completion and signatures
Include notices, amendment process, order of precedence, counterparts/e-signatures (where permitted), governing law/venue after counsel review and signature blocks for both parties. The SOW and signed amendments are part of the contract.
